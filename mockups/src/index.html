The argument

Fourteen changes, and why

These are the problems the mockups answer. Each one is annotated in place on the page where it applies.

01

Eight flat nav items became four groups

The primary nav made the reader scan every label on every page, and still omitted Apply and Contact. Four grouped entries with a descriptor-bearing mega-menu — The Fund, Portfolio, Research, Join — cover all ten destinations.

02

The hero gave back the fold

A 100dvh hero pushed the four proof stats below the fold on every laptop. At 82vh the headline, both actions, and the stat rail land in one screen.

03

A hero variant with no image at all

The skyline is a 1920×1080 largest-paint element rendered at 30% opacity behind a near-opaque scrim. Variant B spends that budget on a live portfolio panel instead — shown side by side so the trade is visible.

04

Proof moved above the fold

Benchmarked to the S&P 500, reported quarterly, faculty-supervised. The credibility argument was arriving three sections too late.

05

Sortable columns, sector filters, a sticky header row, a sparkline per position, and an expanding row that reveals the thesis instead of dead-ending.

A treemap answers "what dominates this book" in one look — and SPY at 41.5% is the answer. Cash, 2.4% and absent from the live allocation view, is in the picture now.

The benchmark is drawn by default

The live chart plots the fund alone and hides the comparison behind a toggle. The spread is now filled between the two lines, and drawdown sits under the same time axis.

Performance

08

Risk figures grouped by question

Twelve tiles in one undifferentiated grid became three groups: return earned, risk taken, and return per unit of risk.

09

The roster behaves like a directory

Search and role filters narrow 31 people in place, and a card opens a slide-over panel rather than a separate route — so a reader mid-scan keeps their place.

10

Open seats are stated, not hidden

A visible gap is a recruiting asset: it tells an applicant exactly where they would land. Twelve open seats across six teams, named.

Recruiting reads as a funnel

A stepper with a live position marker, a deadline countdown, an interview-day checklist, and the two interview rounds separated from the eight events leading up to them.

12

A persistent Apply bar on mobile

Past the hero, the header's Apply button is gone and nothing replaces it. A bottom bar slides in with the deadline attached.

13

A named type scale

Every heading on the live site is a hand-tuned inline clamp(). Six named steps mean sizes get picked rather than invented — and chart colors, previously hardcoded inside the performance route, became tokens.

Desk mode for the data pages

Holdings and Performance are read for long stretches. Both get an opt-in dark register that re-points the same tokens — no component forks, no second stylesheet.

Motion vocabulary

The site's existing classes are carried over intact — the tactile press, the card lift, the left-origin underline wipe, the arrow nudge, and the gold rail that wipes down a table row's left edge instead of transforming it and misaligning columns mid-scan. All on one easing curve, cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1).

Added: scroll-linked progress, an IntersectionObserver reveal with capped stagger, count-ups, a drawing chart line, and the ticker — which was already defined in styles.css and used by no route.

Accessibility

Every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion counterpart: nothing loops, nothing translates, and nothing is left invisible. Content ships visible when JavaScript never runs.

Sorting and filtering are keyboard-operable and announce through aria-sort and aria-pressed. The drawer and the slide-over trap focus, close on Escape, and return focus to the trigger. The gold focus ring is global.

If you want these shipped

What it would take

  1. Pick a hero. Variant A or B. Everything else on the homepage is independent of that choice.
  2. Land the design system first. The type scale, chart tokens, and a real button, card, badge, and table component — the live site installs only nine shadcn primitives and has none of those four, so every CTA repeats a ten-class string.
  3. Then the data pages. Holdings and Performance carry the most benefit and the most work: sorting, filtering, row expansion, treemap, benchmark overlay.
  4. Then navigation and the funnel. The mega-menu, the mobile apply bar, and moving Apply and Contact into the nav.
  5. Re-run the accessibility suite. CI already fails on any new axe violation; these layouts were built to that standard but the real components need to be checked against it.

Nothing under src/ was modified to produce these. They are standalone pages under mockups/, built with node mockups/build.mjs.
