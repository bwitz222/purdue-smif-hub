Key terms

Glossary

Intrinsic Value
What a business is worth based on the cash it will generate, independent of what the market is currently paying.
DCF
Discounted cash flow. Projecting free cash flow and discounting it back at the cost of capital to arrive at intrinsic value.
WACC
Weighted average cost of capital — the blended after-tax cost of a firm's debt and equity, used as the DCF discount rate.
Free Cash Flow
Cash from operations less capital expenditure: what is actually available to debt and equity holders.
Terminal Value
The value of all cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period. Often the majority of a DCF, and the assumption most worth stressing.
EV / EBITDA
Enterprise value over earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization. Capital-structure neutral, which is why it travels better than P/E across a comp set.
Margin of Safety
The gap between price and estimated intrinsic value — the buffer that absorbs being wrong.
Economic Moat
A durable structural advantage that keeps competitors from eroding returns on capital.
Catalyst
The identifiable event or trend expected to close the gap between price and value, and roughly when.
Alpha
Return above what the benchmark and the position's market exposure alone would have delivered.
Beta
Sensitivity to the market. A beta of 1.0 moves with the index; the fund's weighted beta is 1.02.
Sharpe Ratio
Excess return over the risk-free rate divided by volatility — return per unit of risk taken.
